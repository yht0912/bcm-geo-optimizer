# Multi-site Governance

## Intent ownership map

Assign one preferred property and landing page to each commercial intent. Use a table:

| Intent | Audience/market | Primary property | Primary page | Supporting properties | Conflict status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Flag conflicts when multiple owned sites target the same audience, offer, location, and query family without a clear role.

## Site roles

Useful roles include:

- corporate/entity authority;
- product or service conversion;
- regional or language experience;
- documentation or knowledge base;
- editorial education;
- partner or campaign property.

Roles must be visible in content, internal links, entity data, canonicals, and conversion paths.

## Shared evidence registry

Maintain a governed registry of reusable facts:

- claim identifier;
- exact claim and scope;
- responsible owner;
- supporting source;
- effective and review dates;
- allowed sites/languages;
- risk or compliance notes.

Do not silently copy claims across sites when scope, location, eligibility, or freshness differs.

## Cannibalization controls

- consolidate or differentiate duplicate pages;
- use canonical tags only for true equivalents;
- align sitemap and internal links with the preferred page;
- avoid publishing many thin location or question variants;
- track outcomes by property and intent, not only network totals;
- preserve redirects and historical evidence during consolidation.

## Portfolio reporting

Report both network and site-level results. A network-level rise can hide one site's decline or intent conflict. Keep provider, locale, prompt, and property dimensions available for audit.
